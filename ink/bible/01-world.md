# SALT & STATIC — World

## Place
**Umoya Bay** — a fictional coastal town on the KwaZulu-Natal north coast, South Africa.
Present day. Salt-eaten, half-asleep, rain for weeks at a time. Fishing harbour, a
decommissioned aluminium smelter, one road in and out.

## The three fixed points of the town
1. **Kestrel Head** — the headland and its lighthouse. Went dark at **04:40, 14 March 1996**.
   The last keeper's log ends mid-sentence: *"the light is not mine any more, it is —"*
   No bodies, no wreck, no explanation. The lamp room was found sealed from the inside.
2. **The Boathouse** — a converted harbour warehouse that houses **Radio Umoya, AM 1215**.
   The last analogue radio station on the coast. Being shut at month's end; everything
   is migrating to streaming.
3. **The N2 turn-off** — where the town's stories leave and don't come back.

## The signal (the rules)
- 1215 kHz was **given to the station by the lighthouse** in 1996 — the frequency was
  "handed down" when the light died. Nobody at the station today knows what that means.
- The studio has a **second telephone line** that was never connected to anything.
  It rings. Only between **04:38 and 04:42**.
- **Tape is honest.** The station's reels recorded things the operators don't remember saying.
- Anything heard on the dead frequency is **remembered by the sea before it is remembered
  by people.**

## Tone
Quiet, wet, patient. Horror that arrives as weather, not as a monster. The supernatural
is never louder than the rain.

## Rules of the supernatural (for consistency, chapter to chapter)
1. The dead frequency can carry a voice, never a body.
2. The voice knows names. It does not know time.
3. Whatever answers the call **is heard by everyone in the building** — but only the
   person on the phone remembers it afterwards.
4. The lighthouse light is the tell: when it burns, the signal is open.
